### Interpretation 1: “Why do we need to correct errors in a quantum computer? Isn’t nature already quantum mechanical and therefore ‘correct’ by definition?”

Nature is perfectly quantum mechanical. The problem isn’t that quantum mechanics makes mistakes—it’s that **the qubit is leaking**. The perfectly isolated two-rung ladder we engineered is a fragile dream. In reality:

- Stray electric fields, tiny magnetic fluctuations, and thermal vibrations all gently **bump the seesaw**.

### Interpretation 2: “Why do we need to _actively_ apply correction operations? Can’t we just build the qubit better and leave it alone?”

You can build a qubit to be extremely quiet—and we do. But there’s a fundamental limit: if a qubit is perfectly isolated from everything, you can’t control it or read it out. That’s the miracle of quantum error correction: it continuously detects and fixes tiny random nudges, indefinitely, while the computation runs.
