## §1 Predictions

### P1: Spectroscopy Anchoring Holds

**[CHECK: 2028]** By 2028, no peer-reviewed paper will demonstrate that multi-level spectroscopy of superconducting qubits depends circularly on qubit-calibrated measurements in a way that cannot be independently verified.

**If true:** The calibration anchor is real. Qubit subspace is independently identifiable in the bare Hamiltonian.  
**If false:** The entire quantum computing stack loses its operational grounding.  
**Status:** [PENDING]

### P2: GST Gauge Freedom Matters Below 10⁻⁴

**[CHECK: 2030]** By 2030, at least one experiment will demonstrate that gate set tomography's gauge freedom introduces systematic errors exceeding 10% of the estimated error rate at per-gate fidelities below 10⁻⁴.

**If true:** GST-derived error models cannot be trusted for fault-tolerant threshold estimates without independent verification.  
**If false:** GST gauge freedom is operationally irrelevant — self-consistency is sufficient.  
**Status:** [PENDING]

### P3: Calibration Circularity Becomes Measurable at Scale

**[CHECK: 2032]** By 2032, quantum processors with >1,000 physical qubits will exhibit calibration drift patterns that cannot be fully corrected by the calibration procedures that were adequate at <100 qubits.

**If true:** Calibration circularity has a scaling problem — it's negligible for small systems but becomes the limiting factor for large ones.  
**If false:** Calibration is a solved problem; scaling is about qubit fabrication, not metrology.  
**Status:** [PENDING]

### P4: The "Qubit Delusion" Debate Resolves to Pedagogical, Not Operational

**[CHECK: 2030]** By 2030, a consensus will emerge that the particle-qubit picture criticized by "The Qubit Delusion" is primarily a pedagogical and public-communication problem, not an operational one — working engineers already think in terms of modes, collective excitations, and relational observables.

**If true:** The QNFO critique, while valid at the pedagogical level, overstates the operational import. The map-territory confusion is in textbooks and press releases, not in dilution refrigerators.  
**If false:** The ontological confusion genuinely affects hardware design decisions, and the $35B investment was partially misallocated due to the wrong conceptual framework.  
**Status:** [PENDING]

**If true:** The pedagogical bridge this project builds has measurable practical value.

**[CHECK: 2035]** By 2035, logical qubit characterization at the 10⁻⁶ error level will still rely on self-consistent calibration of physical qubits, but the circularity will be bounded by independent checks at the 10⁻³ level (e.g., code-specific benchmarking that doesn't require full physical-qubit calibration).
